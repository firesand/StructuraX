# Repair round after the outside audit of 2026-11-19

Date: 2026-11-19. Scope note for one bounded set of repairs. It does not
change the project status: vendor-runtime acceptance and broad parity remain
unestablished, and no percentage of compatibility is claimed.

An outside audit of the whole program (design, sections, solver, file
formats, API and windows) listed about sixty defects. This note records which
of them were found to be real, what was repaired, what is now refused instead
of computed, and what stays open. The identifiers D.. and G.. are those of
[tutorial-video-progress.md](tutorial-video-progress.md).

No number of the audit is a reference. Every repair has a test whose expected
value is a hand calculation, a closed form or a number the vendor publishes
with its example, and each new test was shown to fail without its change.

## How the claims were checked

Six verifiers took the audit's findings by area and tried to refute each one
against the code of this branch and the vendor material: the lines, the
entry point that reaches them, the page of the vendor manual, and whether
the ledger already had it. Formulas were read from the page image, not from
extracted text; one of the defects below (D85) had come from a radical lost
in text extraction. The result:

- about half of the claims are wrong answers from input the program accepts;
- about a dozen are refuted (list at the end);
- the rest are minor, already in the ledger, deliberate, or features that
  were never built.

A planner then worked each confirmed item out by hand from the vendor pages
and decided, item by item, between building the vendor's rule and refusing
the input by name where the vendor material has no rule. Four implementers
repaired one area each; a reviewer read every source change, merged the four patches
and ran the checks on the merged tree.

## What changes for a model that ran before

Results that move:

- **Steel, AISC.** A pipe under bending and torsion (D85, higher ratio). The
  compression strength of slender boxes, angles, double angles, tees and
  channels (D86, lower), and of I sections with a slender web (D86, slightly
  higher: 506.1 kip on the vendor's Example 002, where 498.1 was reported).
  Double angles and double channels everywhere `t2` is used (D87, D97): `t2`
  is the total width, as the vendor defines it. A passing I section with a
  slender flange now reads "OK" (D91).
- **Steel, EC3.** Class 3 sections take the elastic modulus (D90, higher
  ratio).
- **Concrete.** Column shear with axial force (D94) and at 45° under EC2
  (D95); the minor-axis magnifier of rectangular columns (D96); the
  compression steel of doubly reinforced beams is reported (D99); EC2 beam
  shear with the optimised strut angle, the size factor and the stirrup
  yield in the minimum (D100, D101); the ACI minimum eccentricity (D102).
- **Solver.** V13 and V23 of thin shells and plates (D105, larger by the
  factor that was missing); reactions of supports reached through a
  constraint (D106); response-spectrum cases with more than one direction
  (D107); a history that continues from a nonlinear static case with area
  loads (D108); cables defined by a horizontal tension in cases with other
  loads (D109); models in N and m or N and mm with a fixed link (D110).
- **Import.** Rebar materials and the rebar of concrete sections from native
  files (D114); sections of the tags "2L", "2C", "CR" and "WW" from the older
  library format (D98); a vendor S2K file with area stiffness modifiers
  (D119).
- **Tables of the API.** Numbers read back exactly (D115); the section,
  material, case, pattern and combination tables no longer invent or
  overwrite values (D116 to D118).

Input that ran before and is now refused by name:

- EC3: a Class 4 section, and a tee whose stem is beyond 9 ε in bending
  (D90); a channel, tee, angle, double, solid or general section with
  compression and a moment (D89, G71). I sections, boxes and pipes with
  compression and a moment are designed by the member check described under
  "EC3 members in compression and bending" below.
- An I section with unequal flanges, under AISC and under EC3 with a major
  moment (D88); a pipe more slender than 0.45 E/Fy (D86).
- A response-spectrum case with two functions in one direction (D107), with
  directions combined other than by SRSS (D122), with a modal combination
  other than CQC, SRSS or Absolute, or with a load at an angle (D141; vendor
  Example 1-024 has such a case, "RS-10PCT", which was run as CQC).
- Members with tension or compression limits under axial span loads of both
  signs (D111).
- A native file whose diaphragm constraint is split per level (D113; no
  corpus file has it).
- Native export of a rebar material whose two yield values differ (D114).
- A new section staged in the tables without all its columns (D116).

At the end of the first and second parts one refused member stopped the whole
design run and left no results (D127), and the round had added refusals, the
widest of them a single angle in compression or bending. The third part
(below) ends that: a refused member is recorded with its reason and the other
members are designed.

## EC3 members in compression and bending

The earlier check was a plain sum of the axial and moment ratios, which can
be unconservative (D89). The v19 manual names Annex B (Method 2) of EN
1993-1-1 as the vendor's default and says the method is "not repeated in
this manual"; the v24 manual is the same. The round first refused such
members. The user then accepted the standard itself as the source for this
one item, and the check was built the same day:

- equations 6.61 and 6.62 of EN 1993-1-1 6.3.3(4) per design state, with the
  largest compression and the largest moments of the member, the factors of
  Tables B.1 and B.2 and the moment factors of Table B.3 from the member's
  own moment diagrams; beside them the cross-section check 6.2.1(7) at each
  station, 6.3.1 and 6.3.2;
- for I sections with equal flanges (Table B.2), boxes and pipes (Table B.1;
  pipes with the square root of the sum of the squared moment terms, as the
  vendor manual states), Class 1 to 3;
- on the safe side where the design step lacks information: the larger of
  the table's "uniform" and "concentrated" columns; a moment factor of 1.0
  when the member is braced inside its length (the model holds length ratios,
  not brace positions); the worse of the compression and the bending class.

References: the page images of the standard; the vendor's own IS 800 manual,
which prints the same moment-factor table (its row (e) with a wrong sign);
ECCS publication No. 119, Worked Example 5, whose four factors (0.924,
0.489, 0.961, 0.815) the code reproduces. On a 300 x 300 column, 6 m long,
with 1500 kN and a uniform minor moment of 100 kNm the check gives 1.1375;
the earlier sum gave 0.883. No SAP2000 result for such a member was
available, so agreement with the program itself is not shown. Nothing else
of the standard was added. What remains is G71: other shapes, Method 1, and
the vendor's use of the same equations for tension with bending.

D112 is partial: a constrained degree of freedom that is very soft no longer
makes a nonlinear case "singular", but the dynamic case then needs a looser
tolerance than the default. The cause is the penalty form of the constraints
(D03b).

## Second part: the three unconservative defects (D128 to D130)

The round itself had found three defects that gave unconservative results.
They were repaired the same day, with a planner, two implementers and the
reviewer as before. Sources: the v19 design manuals; the v24 manuals where a
v19 page is misprinted. Nothing of a design standard was added.

**Steel, AISC 360-10 (D128, D129).** Results that move:

- Compression of tees, double angles, channels and double channels now takes
  the lower of flexural and flexural-torsional buckling (manual pp. 3-26 to
  3-30). A tee 6 x 8 x 0.5 x 0.4 of 120 in: 192.0 kip, where 195.7 was
  reported; a slender double angle of the earlier tests: 339.3, where 581.7.
- Flexure of pipes (F8), boxes (F7), channels and double channels (their own
  warping constant, flange buckling, the minor axis), tees and double angles
  (F9: yielding, lateral-torsional, flange and stem buckling). A tee 8 x 4 of
  240 in with the stem in compression: 143.4 kip-in, where 400.9 was reported.
  The strength of a tee follows the sign of the moment at each station. The
  page gives the sign of the lateral-torsional term by "M > 0" and "M < 0"
  without saying which moment decides when the sign changes along the member;
  the lower value is taken for the whole state when the stem is compressed at
  any station.
- A passing section of these shapes reads "OK"; "SLENDER" remains for an I
  with a slender web and the built-up I types.

Refused by name under AISC, where they ran before (G81 to G83): a single
angle in compression or bending (the vendor designs it about principal axes
the model does not hold; tension alone is designed); a box with a moment about
an axis whose flange is slender; a channel or double channel in major bending
whose web is beyond the compact limit; these four shapes without a torsional
constant. Until the third part one such member stopped the steel design of
the whole model (D127).

Seven places of the v19 pages are misprinted; each decision is in
`design-fixes.md`. Two rest on the v24 manual, which prints the corrected
form: the modulus of the compression flange in the flange buckling of tees,
and the stem buckling of tees (2.55 - 1.84, and the sign of the moment). The
shear centre, centroid and warping constant are computed from the plate
dimensions (G86); the channel formula reproduces the shear centres of the 64
channels of the vendor's libraries within 0.82 %. Built as printed although
odd: slender box webs, and a jump in the flange buckling of double angles.

**Concrete torsion (D130).** Rectangular beams are designed for torsion as
the manuals print it:

- ACI 318-14 (pp. 3-42 to 3-46): ignored up to φT_th; minimum longitudinal
  steel up to φT_cr (the vendor's printed choice; ACI asks for more); above it
  A_t/s and A_l at 45°, the combined stirrup minimum and the section limit.
- Eurocode 2 (pp. 3-32 to 3-37): ignored while Eq. 6.31 holds; otherwise the
  45° strut in the shear design, A_t/s, A_sl and Eq. 6.29.
- The planner had proposed to refuse Eurocode 2 torsion. The reviewer built
  it instead, because both manuals print every step and a refusal would have
  taken the concrete results of most 3D frames. The oddity of the printed rule
  is D148: with the shear above V_Rd,c, any torsion above this program's noise
  floor gives the 45° strut and a torsion stirrup that does not shrink with
  the torsion. On the safe side; not checked in the vendor program.
- How far that reaches was measured on a test frame (3 storeys, 3 by 2 bays,
  51 beams 300 x 500, live load on some bays; not in the repository): every
  beam takes the rule, although the largest torsion is 2.2 kNm against a
  T_Rd,c of 21 kNm. The shear stirrups rise by 27 % on average and by 68 % at
  most, and the torsion stirrups are of the same size again: 2.4 times the
  transverse steel in total. Whether the vendor program does the same is not
  known. The user decided on 2026-11-19 to keep the rule as printed (D148).
- A model whose beams carry torsion gets more stirrups in the shear column
  under Eurocode 2 (45° instead of the optimised angle) and new torsion
  values. The torsion values are not displayed yet (D146).
- T beams are refused only above the threshold the manual prints for them,
  other shapes at any torsion (G85). Columns keep their results and get a
  note: the vendor designs torsion for beams only.

## Third part: a refused member no longer stops a design run (D127)

SAP2000 reports design error messages per frame (v19 API help,
GetSummaryResults, "ErrorSummary") and designs the other members. The design
runs that the command line, the windows and the API call now do the same:

- A member the design refuses is recorded with the text of its error in a
  list beside the results (`steel_design_errors`, `concrete_design_errors`)
  and the run goes on. A refused member is never among the results, so it
  cannot read as a pass. A rerun, a new analysis or an edit clears the list
  with the results.
- Auto Select passes over a refused candidate and notes it in the result; if
  no section of the list can be designed, that frame alone is refused and
  keeps no choice from an earlier run.
- Errors of the whole run still stop it and leave nothing: an unknown code
  name, a combination without results, missing or non-finite forces, an
  invalid overwrite.
- The command line prints the refused members and their reasons; the status
  line and the side panel of the windows name them; the API has
  `get_error_summary`; the three design tables have rows "Not designed" with
  the message. "Verify all members passed" counts a refused member as not
  passed.
- The library keeps strict entry points (`try_run_steel_design`,
  `try_run_concrete_design`) that stop at the first refused member, as before;
  the earlier tests of the refusals use them unchanged.

Reference of the tests: the member that is designed has bitwise the result of
the same member designed alone. Left open: the design result windows say "no
results" when every member is refused (D149).

## Defects found during the round and left open

Found by the planners and the implementers, not by the audit. Two give
unconservative results today:

- **D142** An I section with a noncompact web is designed with the plastic
  moment (the vendor's F4 forms are not built) and reads "OK".
- **D144** A concrete beam that is not rectangular is designed as the
  rectangle of its outer dimensions: the shear width of a T beam is its
  flange.
- D126 (EC3 Class 3 under high shear, as the manual prints it), D131 (EC2
  α_cc), D132 (column magnifier details, σ_cp), D143
  (no lateral-torsional buckling of solid rectangles), D145, D146 (torsion
  values not displayed), D147 (ACI shear φ for seismic combinations).

Recorded from the audit without a repair: D133 to D140, G78, G79, and an
addition to D25. D138 (what the native writer still writes differently from
vendor files, and the rebar record it writes since D114) can only be settled
by opening such a file in the vendor program; D148 likewise by a run there.

## Claims that were refuted

- Hinges "ignored" in a nonlinear history case: that case type is the
  vendor's nonlinear modal history, in which hinges do not act (Analysis
  Reference p. 148). What differs is the method of integration (D136).
- Nodal stresses of combinations taken from averaged values: the code
  derives them again from the combined forces.
- EC3 shear checked without separating V2 and V3: the vendor reports them
  separately, as the code does.
- "Two steel design paths" in the windows; the shear hinge under P-Delta
  losing a couple; the solid material angle having the wrong sign (it follows
  the manual; the joint angle is the open question, D135); the value of `m_GuideDir`
  in the native writer.
- The audit's worked numbers for EC3 beam-columns could not be reproduced;
  the defect itself is real (D89), with other numbers.

## Not verified

- Nothing was run in the vendor program. Agreement with SAP2000 rests on its
  manuals and published examples only.
- The windows were not opened; their repairs are tested through the code of
  the windows without a display.
- The two lines of the command line that leave cables out of design have no
  test. EC2 doubly reinforced beams above fck 50 MPa keep the earlier form
  and have no test.
- D105: the two strip tests bend one way with ν = 0 and one element across
  the width; no plate that bends two ways was checked. Example 2-008 on the
  20 x 20 mesh is an ignored (slow) test and was not run.
- No test covers a model in which the new link penalty meets buckling.
- Second part: the reviewer read the manual pages of the tee, the channel
  table, the slender-tee equation and both torsion chapters, and the v24 pages
  cited; the box and pipe pages and the nonslender tee equation were read by
  the planner and the implementer only.
- Second part: no vendor example covers flexural-torsional buckling, the
  flexure of these shapes or torsion, so every reference is a hand
  calculation from the manual's formulas. No corpus model runs AISC design on
  these shapes. The side of the flange of a tee (+2 face) follows the vendor's
  drawing; no code or file fixes it. The warping constant of the double
  channel is derived here, checked by a numerical integration; the vendor's
  own value is not known.
